Encoder LR bottleneck: a from-scratch price encoder starved at the LoRA rate

Date 2026-06-04 Component Price Encoder / Training Loop Status Failed Time ~2 h (baseline run + diagnosis)

Summary

First end-to-end JEPA + VICReg baseline on real price data (transformer price encoder, Qwen2.5-1.5B + LoRA predictor). Training loss fell smoothly, but validation quietly diverged from the start: val_jepa drifted up (0.066 → 0.092) while held-out representation variance val z_std drifted down (0.69 → 0.53) — a textbook "the regularizer is satisfied on the train batch but the representation isn't genuinely diverse on unseen data" signature. Root cause is not collapse and not the EMA: the price encoder is the transformer backend (randomly initialised, trained from scratch), but it only unfreezes in Phase 2 and shares one optimizer group with the Qwen LoRA at lr_adapter = 3e-5. That rate is meant to gently nudge a pretrained LM; it barely moves random weights in ~1.5k steps, so z_price stays close to random features and the only thing really learning is the projection head — which then overfits the train batch. Fix implemented: give the encoder backbone its own higher lr_encoder = 1e-4 on a per-group warmup→cosine schedule.

Hypothesis / Motivation

Expectation going in: with VICReg (V+C) holding variance up and the phased schedule warming up projections before adapters, the JEPA objective should pull a useful temporal representation out of the price encoder, and validation JEPA should track training JEPA downward. The run was meant to be a clean baseline to compare VICReg vs the soft-codebook bottleneck later.

Analysis & Findings

Decomposing the total loss: L = jepa + 25·v + 1·c. By mid-run that is roughly 0.08 + 0.25 + 1.6 ≈ 1.93 — the covariance term dominates and is grinding down slowly, while JEPA collapsed to ~0.05 within 50 steps. A JEPA objective that becomes this easy this fast means the prediction target carries little structure to learn: with a near-random frozen-ish encoder on both the online and EMA sides, matching z_pred to z_target is cheap, and VICReg's unit-variance hinge is met by the projection head on the training batch alone.

The mechanism, precisely: tunable_backbone_parameters() for the transformer encoder is dumped into _adapter_params(), which the trainer put in a single optimizer group at lr_adapter = 3e-5 alongside the LoRA adapters, and that group only switches on at Phase 2. So for the first 500 steps the encoder is frozen (projection-only learning on random features), and for the remaining steps it trains at a rate calibrated for a pretrained LM — far too small to move a randomly-initialised 4-layer transformer. The encoder never escapes its random-init basin; the projection head does all the work and overfits.

VICReg, the EMA target, the phased schedule, and the data pipeline all work as intended — train loss descends cleanly and the Phase 1→2 transition is stable. The mechanics are sound.
The diagnosis points at the learning rate as the binding constraint, but a higher encoder LR is a hypothesis. Rising val_jepa could also reflect too few distinct market regimes in the data or VICReg failing to hold diversity off-train — to be checked with a PCA/UMAP of val z_price after the fix.
A from-scratch price encoder trained at the LoRA rate (3e-5) does not learn a usable representation: held-out JEPA degrades and held-out variance shrinks. This baseline config is not a valid representation-learning run.

Thought Process & Debugging Notes

First wrong guess was EMA: a too-high decay can make the target track the context so closely that matching is trivial. Checked config.py — decay is 0.998, squarely in the healthy [0.996, 0.999] band — so that theory died. Second wrong guess was "frozen random backbone": freeze_backbone defaults to True (a sensible default back when the backbone was pretrained Moirai), so I assumed the encoder never trained. But reading the notebook config cell showed it already passes freeze_backbone=False — the backbone does train. The real, narrower cause only became visible by following the param into the optimizer: unfrozen, yes, but in the LoRA's 3e-5 group and only from Phase 2. Lesson to past self: trace the parameter all the way into its optimizer group before theorising; "frozen" vs "trainable" is not binary when the learning rate is three times too small.

Lessons Learned

  • The transformer price backend is trained from scratch; it must not inherit the pretrained-LM LoRA learning rate. Give it its own, higher rate.
  • VICReg's variance hinge is a training-batch constraint — train z_std ≈ 1 with val z_std falling means apparent health masking real over-fit. Always watch the held-out z_std, not just the train one.
  • "Unfreeze the backbone" and "let the backbone actually learn" are different claims separated by the learning rate. Verify the optimizer group, not just requires_grad.
  • A JEPA loss that crashes to near-zero in tens of steps is a warning, not a win — it usually means the target is too easy to predict.

Next Steps

  1. Done — implemented: split the encoder backbone into its own optimizer group with lr_encoder = 1e-4 (separate from LoRA's 3e-5), on a per-group warmup→cosine schedule (lr_warmup_steps = 100, lr_min_factor = 0.1) so the from-scratch backbone ramps in smoothly at Phase 2 and decays late for stability.
  2. On the value 1e-4: it's a deliberately conservative starting point for a 4-layer from-scratch transformer — roughly 3× the old LoRA rate, enough to actually move random weights, but low enough to keep the Phase-2 transition stable rather than risk a second failure mode (a hot encoder thrashing the EMA target it's also feeding). The reasoning: change one variable at a time, and pick the smaller of two plausible rates first so a clean re-run isolates "was the LR the bottleneck?" from "did we overshoot?". If the re-run's val_jepa still looks sluggish (slow to turn over, or turning but shallow) with a healthy lr_enc trace, lr_encoder = 2e-4 is the next thing to try before reaching for structural changes.
  3. Re-run the baseline with the new schedule; confirm val_jepa now decreases and val z_std holds. Log the LR trace (lr_enc) that the trainer now prints.
  4. If val still degrades with a healthy encoder LR (i.e. 1e-4, and a 2e-4 bump didn't help either), the LR was not the binding constraint and the next suspect is the data — too few distinct market regimes for the representation to separate. Per the entry's open question above, run a PCA/UMAP of val z_price to see whether held-out embeddings cluster by regime or smear into one blob, and compare against the soft-codebook bottleneck.
  5. Consider whether the encoder should train from Phase 1 (no warmup gap) rather than waiting for step 500.
