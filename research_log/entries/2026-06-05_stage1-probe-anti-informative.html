Stage 1 Linear Probe: nb03_best.pt is anti-informative

Date 2026-06-05 Component Evaluation / Price Encoder / VICReg Status Failed Time ~1 h (eval only, no training)

Summary

Stage 0 diagnostics (nb00) had already returned degenerate on nb03_best.pt — the shuffled-target control gave a ratio of 0.999 (true cosine 0.616 vs shuffled cosine 0.615), meaning the predictor is cosine-similar to every target window, not the correct one. Stage 1 was supposed to quantify whatever downstream value remained. Instead it escalated the diagnosis: the trained encoder is not merely empty, it is anti-informative on validation.

Across all three probe targets, a frozen untrained random encoder of the identical architecture decodes the future better than the trained checkpoint. The cleanest signal is future volatility — the easiest target, since volatility clusters and is autocorrelated: an untrained random projection hits val R² +0.097, while the trained encoder manages only +0.058. Training removed ~40% of the volatility signal a random projection carried for free. On direction, the trained model sits exactly on the majority-class baseline (0.528) while untrained is 3 pp above chance with AUROC 0.569. Because the backbone is frozen (freeze_backbone=True), the only trained parameter in the encoder is the projection head — so the entire degradation is attributable to the head, and the val-only nature of the gap (train R² is comparable) points at the JEPA+VICReg objective pushing the head toward a cosine-tidy but downstream-useless manifold.

Hypothesis / Motivation

Per CLAUDE.md metric discipline, the downstream linear probe — not val_jepa — is the primary progress metric. val_jepa can be low via EMA collapse (context and EMA-target projection heads converging to similar mappings regardless of input window), so a low val_jepa must be cleared by the shuffled-target control before it counts as progress. Stage 0 had not cleared it. The motivating question for Stage 1:

How much task signal does z_price from nb03_best.pt carry,
relative to an untrained random encoder of the same architecture?

H1: trained z_price linearly decodes future return / volatility / direction better than untrained by more than sampling noise → the encoder learned something useful. H0: trained ≈ untrained within noise on all three → training added no decodable signal. Given the Stage 0 verdict, we expected H0 (trained ≈ untrained). We did not expect trained to come in measurably below untrained.

Analysis & Findings

The volatility number is the smoking gun

Volatility is the easiest of the three targets: it clusters in time and is strongly autocorrelated, so even a fixed random projection of normalized log-returns linearly decodes it (untrained val R² +0.097). If training were merely neutral — adding no signal — we'd expect trained ≈ untrained here. Instead the trained encoder lands at +0.058, having destroyed ~40% of the signal a random projection carried for free. A target this forgiving regressing is the strongest possible statement that the representation got worse, not just flat.

Why this is attributable to the projection head specifically

The checkpoint was trained with freeze_backbone=True. In TransformerPriceEncoder the forward is input_proj → +CLS → +pos → backbone → pooled = h[:,0] → head(pooled) → z_price, and input_proj, the CLS token, and the transformer stack are all frozen. The only parameter that moved during nb03 is head (the ProjectionHead). Therefore the difference between trained-z_price and untrained-z_price is entirely the difference between a trained head and a random head on top of the same frozen random features. The val-only degradation (train R² is comparable) means the head learned a mapping that fits train statistics under the JEPA+VICReg objective but does not transport — consistent with the head being pulled toward the EMA-collapse manifold the shuffled control already flagged.

What failed: the frozen-random-backbone + trained-head + JEPA/VICReg configuration is anti-informative. The trained projection head actively discards downstream signal that survives a random projection. val_jepa = 0.384 is a mirage produced by EMA collapse, exactly as CLAUDE.md warned.
The insight: a low val_jepa with a flat-or-negative probe is the textbook EMA-collapse signature. The intrinsic metric (cosine to a moving EMA target) can be driven down by making the two projection heads agree on any input — which is precisely a head that throws away input-specific structure. The probe and the shuffled control are measuring the same failure from two directions, and they agree.
Resolved by the follow-up below. The original open question — is the frozen backbone carrying signal the head destroys, or is the backbone also empty? — is answered by the pre-head (backbone pooled) probe: both. The trained head removes ~0.023 of the volatility R² its own backbone preserved (a random head removes none), AND the frozen backbone is itself weak (~0.08). See "Follow-up: backbone probe" below.

Follow-up: backbone (pre-head) probe — head-vs-backbone resolved

nb03c Test 4 re-runs the volatility probe on the pre-projection representation — the pooled CLS hidden state fed into price_encoder.head, captured via a forward pre-hook (probe_source="backbone") — for both models. Comparing pre-head vs post-head within each model isolates the head's effect, and because each delta uses that model's own backbone as its reference, it is robust to the two frozen backbones being different random draws.

The trained head is the destroyer. It removes 0.023 of volatility R² its own backbone preserved; a random head removes essentially none (−0.0001). The destruction is a property of what JEPA+VICReg+EMA trained the head to do — collapse toward an input-independent, cosine-tidy z_price — not of the backbone's features.
But the backbone is weak too — a pretrained one is necessary, not sufficient. The frozen random backbone carries only ~0.08 volatility R² (a random projection of raw features, no financial inductive bias). Two problems, not one: (1) a weak backbone, fixed by a pretrained model (TimesFM); (2) a collapsing head/loss, which a pretrained backbone does not fix — feed richer features into the same loop and the head will still scramble them on val. Both must be addressed.

Caveats: single target (volatility — the most decodable), single seed, no confidence intervals. The cross-model backbone gap (0.0816 vs 0.0969) is two different frozen random inits — within init-lottery noise, not a training effect (the backbone never receives gradients). The load-bearing numbers are the within-model head-destruction deltas (+0.023 trained vs ~0 untrained), which that noise does not touch. Running the return and direction backbone probes would harden the conclusion, but the volatility result is already directionally decisive and consistent with Stage 0 / Stage 1.

Thought Process & Debugging Notes

First instinct on seeing the deltas was to suspect the probe harness — a negative R² is alarming until you remember it's an honest out-of-sample R² (baseline = predict the val mean), so negative simply means "worse than predicting the mean", which is a real and possible outcome for a degenerate representation. The untrained baseline being run through the identical harness is the control that rules out a probe bug: if the harness were broken, untrained would look broken too, and it doesn't — untrained produces sensible, mildly-positive numbers.

The temptation was to immediately reach for the CLAUDE.md "tune lambda_c upward to fix collapse" lever. But that lever only touches z_price marginal statistics (per-dim variance, off-diagonal decorrelation); it cannot fix a representation that has thrown away input geometry, and it would burn Colab units producing a prettier effective-rank number on a still-useless embedding. The probe being anti-informative is a stronger stop signal than any intrinsic metric: no hyperparameter on this architecture is worth sweeping until the encoder beats a random projection on at least one downstream target.

The one framing correction worth recording: the decisive follow-up is not "trained backbone vs untrained backbone" (the frozen backbone is the same random extractor in both, so that delta is ~0 by construction). It is "pre-head pooled vs post-head z_price, within each model" — that isolates whether the head is the destroyer.

Lessons Learned

  • Always run the untrained-encoder probe in the same harness. It is both the decision baseline and the harness sanity check. Sensible untrained numbers + bad trained numbers = the model is the problem, not the probe.
  • Anti-informative beats "no signal" as a stop condition. A trained model that scores below a random projection on the easiest target (volatility) is not a tuning problem — it is an architecture/objective problem. Do not sweep hyperparameters past this point.
  • With a frozen backbone, the probe gap localizes to the head automatically. Knowing which parameters were trainable turns an ambiguous "the model is bad" into a precise "the projection head is destroying signal", which directly changes what to fix.
  • val-only degradation with comparable train R² is the EMA-collapse fingerprint. It is the downstream-side echo of the shuffled-target control failing — two independent diagnostics pointing at the same root cause is as strong as a verdict gets here.

Next Steps

  1. Backbone probe — DONE (see follow-up above). The trained head destroys signal a random head does not (+0.023 vs ~0 vol R²), and the frozen backbone is itself weak (~0.08). Conclusion: a pretrained backbone is necessary but not sufficient — both the backbone and the head/loss must change.
  2. Establish the backbone ceiling cheaply — no JEPA. Before any training, probe RAW pretrained-backbone features (TimesFM pooled, no projection head, no JEPA) on vol / return / direction. Forward-passes only (~no Colab budget). De-risks the pivot: confirm vol R² lands well above the ~0.08 random-backbone floor before committing. TimesFM first — no numpy<2 dependency; Moirai only in a dedicated numpy<2 env.
  3. Then fix the loop, not just the backbone. Head destruction is independent of backbone quality, so the JEPA/VICReg+EMA formulation needs a change that gives the head a reason to preserve input structure: reconstruction pretraining before JEPA, the soft-codebook bottleneck (CLAUDE.md open decision) in place of VICReg, or dropping the trained projection on the target side. Pick one and probe it against this entry's floor (untrained random ≈ +0.097 vol R², ≈ 0.559 direction acc).
  4. No new training run on this architecture until the backbone probe clears the head-vs-backbone question. Per CLAUDE.md, a change that cannot beat the shuffled control / probe baseline is not a forward step.
  5. Record the result as the Stage 1 floor. Whatever a pretrained backbone scores later is compared against these numbers (untrained random ≈ +0.097 vol R², ≈ 0.559 direction acc) — not against val_jepa.
